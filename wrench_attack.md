## Conference notes May 10th — talk description: José Pedro Sousa

> *Card description:* Yeah but your security is not safety: solving the $5 wrench attack problem
>
> You've implemented end-to-end encryption. Your keys never touch a server. You're using ZK proofs, stealth addresses, maybe even computing over encrypted values on-chain. Your privacy stack is airtight. Then someone shows up with a $5 wrench. This attempts to answer a simple question: where does the trust ultimately live, and can someone get there with a wrench?

Previously at Polygon, I've been designing and integrating zero-knowledge products since 2022. I have an unhealthy obsession with what happens to your private keys when someone shows up in person.

## Conference notes May 10th — wallet in space / TE in orbit

> *Sylvia:* So the idea here is quite simple to generalize, and it's a bit overwhelming, I guess, because we think of satellites and everything. It's not such a big deal.
>
> What we do in Space Computers is, technically, we enforce these policies in a TE, and then we just shoot the TE into space so it's out of reach. When we execute these policies, they execute in the TE, and you avoid all problems of side attacks on the TEs. I guess some of us know that they are kind of not very reliable, but if you put them in orbit, they are out of reach also for these kinds of physical attacks.
