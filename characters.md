## The curly-haired man with the dead phone

### May 10th journal — phone dead, spinning in circles

> OH, that my phone died on the way back to my hostel, as I got off of the tram. I had not yet gone to the hostel using the tram, so I stood there for a moment, spinning in a circle, trying to work out which way to go. I recognised nothing. I reached my phone to check, stared at the blank screen, put it away again. My brain could not comprehend that I was alone, in a foreign city, without a map and without a phone.
>
> I walked around the intersection, then set off in the opposite direction, convinced that that was possibly where the restaurant was that I ate at the night before, and if it was, I had at least a chance of finding my way home.
>
> The restaurant did not appear.
>
> I took a deep breath and turned left. Another junction that I felt unreasonably certain that I had never seen before.
>
> Curly hair, earbuds, phone out, waiting at the lights.
>
> I saw a curly-haired man with his phone out, waiting for the lights. "Excuse me," I said. He removed an earbud. "Do you speak English?"
>
> "Of course," he said, but with a smile.
>
> "Could you do me a huge favour and pull up Sophie's Hostel on your map and tell me which way to go?"
>
> The light changed. He started to cross as he typed. Sophie's? Ah yes. Oh. It's not here.
>
> Possibly I got off at the wrong tram stop, a possibility which had not previously occurred to me.
>
> "OK." He showed me the map. "So you have to go straight, then turn right, then left, then.... Oh dear, I fear this is too difficult."
>
> I was almost on tiptoe trying to see his screen. I could follow the first three steps easily, I thought, and maybe by then I would recognise my surroundings.
>
> "I think I can do it," I told him.
>
> "It would be easier if you went by tram. Do you have a tranpsort ticket?"
>
> "Yes!" His face brightened. Thenn I thought it through. "But it is on my phoe, which is dead." His face fell.
>
> "Show me again."
>
> The lights changed and changed again.
>
> He showed me the route, pointing to show me. "Up here, past the park, turn right. Then you will turn left. Uphill. You must walk uphill, not downhill."
>
> I nodded. "I think I can do this this. I think I will recognise the turn where my hostel is."
>
> He looked dubious. If I was thatgood at following directions, how had I gotten myself into this situationin the first place?
>
> "Look at the map one more time," he commanded. I did. "It'll be fine," I told him.
>
> He did not think it would be fine. I suspected he was debating whether he should walk me there to deliver me safely in person. It was the opposite direction of whereever he was going.
>
> I looked at the map again, reciting the instructions again as reassuringly as I could. "I've got it. Past the park, turn uphill, continue a few blocks, find the hostel." I nodded. The lights turned green. "I've got it," I said and stepped that way.
>
> "Good luck," he said, clearly of the belief I needed it.
>
> I followed the instructions. I did not recognisethe intersection where my hostel was. However, looking right at each intersection, I spotted the sign for my hostel. I'd made it.
>
> I thought for a moment that I should have given the man mytelegram handle, taken ascreenshot of his contact details, soI could message him to say I was safe. No phone, of course I coudln't.
